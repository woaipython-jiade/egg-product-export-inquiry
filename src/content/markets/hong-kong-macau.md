## Market Access Overview

Hong Kong and Macau are the most accessible export destinations for Chinese egg products. Both SARs maintain open import policies with well-defined regulatory frameworks.

**Hong Kong** regulates egg product imports under **Cap. 132AK — Imported Game, Meat, Poultry and Eggs Regulations**. All imported eggs and egg products require an official health certificate and must enter through designated control points.

**Macau** regulates food imports through the **Municipal Affairs Bureau (IAM)**, requiring import licenses submitted at least 3 working days before shipment arrival.

### Key Advantages

- **No quota restrictions** on egg product imports from mainland China
- **Geographic proximity** — same-day delivery via Man Kam To / Lok Ma Chau land crossings
- **Established regulatory framework** with published requirements
- **Large consumer market** — HK imports ~90% of its food supply

## Hong Kong: Regulatory Requirements

### Governing Legislation

| Legislation | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| **Cap. 132AK** — Imported Game, Meat, Poultry and Eggs Regulations | Controls import of eggs and egg products; requires health certificates |
| **Cap. 132W** — Food and Drugs (Composition and Labelling) Regulations | Mandatory food labeling requirements |
| **Cap. 132AN** — Food Business Regulation | Registration of food importers/distributors |

### Import Procedure

1. **Importer registration**: The HK importer must register with the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (FEHD) using **Form FEHB 295** — "Application for Permission to Import Game, Meat, Poultry or Eggs." Registration fee: **HK$195**, valid for **3 years**.

2. **Health certificate**: Each consignment requires a health certificate issued by Chinese customs authorities (GACC/local customs) confirming the products are fit for human consumption, free from disease, and produced under hygienic conditions.

3. **Entry point**: Egg products from mainland China primarily enter through **Man Kam To Food Control Office** for land shipments or the designated cold store inspection points for sea freight.

4. **CFS surveillance testing**: The Centre for Food Safety conducts risk-based surveillance sampling at import. Products may be sampled for microbiological, chemical, and contaminant testing.

### Food Safety Standards

The CFS enforces the following limits for egg products:

**Microbiological Standards** (per CFS Guidelines for Microbiological Quality of Ready-to-Eat Food):

| Parameter | Limit |
|-----------|-------|
| Salmonella | Not detected in 25g |
| Listeria monocytogenes | Not detected in 25g (for ready-to-eat products) |
| E. coli (generic) | <20 CFU/g (satisfactory) |
| Aerobic plate count | <10⁴ CFU/g (satisfactory) |

**Chemical Contaminants & Residues**:

| Parameter | Limit | Reference |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Melamine | 2.5 mg/kg (adults), 1 mg/kg (infants) | Cap. 132CM |
| Lead | 0.5 mg/kg | Cap. 132V, Schedule 1 |
| Veterinary drug residues | Per Codex Alimentarius / China GB 31650 MRLs | CFS risk assessment |
| Sudan dyes (I–IV) | Not permitted | CFS targeted surveillance |

### Labeling Requirements (Cap. 132W)

All pre-packaged egg products sold in HK must display:
- Product name (in English and Chinese)
- List of ingredients (in descending order of weight)
- "Best before" or "Use by" date
- Storage conditions
- Name and address of manufacturer/packer/importer
- Country of origin
- Net weight/volume
- Allergen declaration (eggs are a major allergen)

## Macau: Regulatory Requirements

### Governing Authority

The **Municipal Affairs Bureau (IAM)** — Food Safety Division regulates all food imports into Macau.

### Import Procedure

1. **Import license application**: The Macau importer must submit an import application to IAM **at least 3 working days** before the goods arrive. Applications are submitted through IAM's food import notification system.

2. **Required documents for IAM**:
   - Health certificate from Chinese customs authorities
   - Certificate of origin
   - Product test report (if requested by IAM)
   - Commercial invoice and packing list

3. **Inspection**: IAM may conduct inspection and sampling at the border checkpoint. Products failing inspection are refused entry or destroyed.

### Labeling Requirements

Macau requires food labels in **at least one of the official languages** (Chinese or Portuguese). In practice, Chinese-language labels are accepted. Labels must include:
- Product name
- Ingredients list
- Net quantity
- Production and expiry dates
- Storage conditions
- Name/address of producer and importer
- Country of origin

## Risk Notes

- **Melamine testing** remains a routine check for all egg products from mainland China — this is a legacy of the 2008 melamine incident and continues as standard CFS surveillance protocol
- **Sudan dye screening** is included in CFS targeted surveillance for egg products — ensure production chain has no contact with Sudan dyes
- **CFS publishes monthly food surveillance results** — non-compliant products are publicly named, which can damage trade relationships
- **Macau's IAM** may impose additional requirements during food safety incidents or disease outbreaks — monitor IAM announcements
- **Avian influenza (HPAI)** outbreaks in mainland China can trigger temporary suspensions from affected provinces

## Action Checklist

- [ ] Confirm GACC registration is current for your facility (GACC Decree 248/249)
- [ ] Obtain health certificate from local customs office for each consignment
- [ ] Ensure HK importer has registered with FEHD (Form FEHB 295, HK$195/3yr)
- [ ] Prepare bilingual (EN/ZH) labels complying with Cap. 132W
- [ ] Test products against CFS microbiological and chemical limits before shipment
- [ ] Specifically test for melamine (<2.5 mg/kg) and Sudan dyes (not detected)
- [ ] For Macau: ensure importer submits IAM application ≥3 working days before arrival
- [ ] Arrange cold chain logistics for temperature-sensitive products
- [ ] Monitor CFS monthly surveillance reports for any enforcement trends
