## Market Access Overview

The United States presents a **structurally closed** market for Chinese egg products. The primary barrier is the **USDA FSIS equivalence determination** under the **Egg Products Inspection Act (EPIA)**, which China has not obtained. This means Chinese egg products **cannot legally enter the US market for human consumption**.

Beyond the regulatory barrier, **Section 301 tariffs** on Chinese goods add significant cost even if technical access were granted.

---

## The Legal Barrier: FSIS Equivalence

### Egg Products Inspection Act (EPIA)

The **Egg Products Inspection Act** (21 USC §1031–1056) requires that:
- All egg products sold in the US must be inspected by USDA FSIS or an equivalent foreign system
- Foreign countries exporting egg products to the US must have their inspection systems determined **equivalent** to the US system by FSIS
- This is codified in **9 CFR Part 590** — Inspection of Eggs and Egg Products

### FSIS Equivalence Determination

FSIS maintains a list of countries with equivalence determinations for various product categories. For egg products specifically:

- **China status**: **NOT EQUIVALENT** — China has not received an FSIS equivalence determination for egg products
- **Countries with egg product equivalence**: Very few countries have this — check the current FSIS list
- **FSIS equivalence information**: [https://www.fsis.usda.gov/inspection/import-export/import-export-library](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/inspection/import-export/import-export-library)

### What Equivalence Would Require

| Step | Requirement | Timeline |
|------|------------|----------|
| 1. Formal request | China's competent authority requests FSIS equivalence review | Initiation |
| 2. Self-evaluation | China submits detailed documentation of its egg product inspection system | 6–12 months |
| 3. Document review | FSIS reviews China's legal framework, inspection procedures, laboratory systems | 12–24 months |
| 4. On-site audit | FSIS conducts audit mission to China — inspects facilities, labs, government oversight | 6–12 months |
| 5. Final rule | FSIS publishes proposed and final rule in Federal Register | 12–24 months |
| **Total** | | **3–6+ years** |

## FDA Requirements (Concurrent with FSIS)

Even if FSIS equivalence were obtained, FDA requirements also apply:

### FDA Facility Registration (FURLS)

Under the **Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act** (21 USC §350d) and **21 CFR Part 1, Subpart H**:
- All foreign food facilities must register with FDA through the **FURLS** (FDA Unified Registration and Listing System)
- Registration URL: [https://www.fda.gov/food/online-registration-food-facilities](https://www.fda.gov/food/online-registration-food-facilities)
- Biennial renewal required
- US Agent must be designated

### Prior Notice (21 CFR Part 1, Subpart I)

- **Prior notice** must be filed with FDA before food arrives at the US port of entry
- Filing deadline: varies by transport mode (15 days for ocean freight, 4 hours for truck)
- Filed through ACE (Automated Commercial Environment) or FDA Prior Notice System Interface

### FSVP — Foreign Supplier Verification Program (21 CFR Part 1, Subpart L)

Under FSMA (Food Safety Modernization Act):
- The **US importer** must conduct hazard analysis and verify that the foreign supplier's food safety controls are equivalent to US requirements
- Importer must maintain verification activities including on-site audits, testing, or review of supplier records
- **21 CFR 1.500–1.514** contains FSVP requirements
- FSVP information: [https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-foreign-supplier-verification-programs-fsvp-importers-food-humans-and-animals](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-foreign-supplier-verification-programs-fsvp-importers-food-humans-and-animals)

### Labeling Requirements (21 CFR Part 101)

If US market access were obtained, egg products would need:
- English-language labeling
- Nutrition Facts panel (21 CFR 101.9)
- Allergen declaration — "Contains: Egg" under FALCPA (Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act)
- Country of origin: "Product of China"
- USDA inspection mark (if FSIS-inspected)

### Section 301 Tariffs on Chinese Goods

**Critical**: Chinese egg products would be subject to **Section 301 tariffs** imposed on Chinese-origin goods:
- Multiple tranches of tariffs have been imposed since 2018
- Current effective tariff rate on Chinese food products can reach **25% or higher** on top of MFN rates
- Section 301 tariff list: [https://ustr.gov/issue-areas/enforcement/section-301-investigations](https://ustr.gov/issue-areas/enforcement/section-301-investigations)
- Even if regulatory access were obtained, these tariffs would make Chinese egg products **price-uncompetitive** in the US market

## Risk Notes

- **Do not plan US exports as a feasible near-term strategy** — FSIS equivalence is a multi-year process with **no current indication of progress**
- **Dual regulatory gate**: Both USDA FSIS (egg inspection) AND FDA (facility registration, FSVP) requirements must be met — one is not sufficient without the other
- **Section 301 tariffs are substantial** — even with regulatory access, competitive pricing would be extremely challenging
- **US-China trade relations** add political uncertainty to any regulatory progress
- **FDA FSMA requirements** (FSVP) place significant compliance burden on the US importer — this may discourage importers from pursuing Chinese egg products even if access were technically available
- The **Egg Products Inspection Act** predates modern trade arrangements and creates a higher bar than many other food categories

---

## Strategic Considerations

- Monitor USDA FSIS equivalence determinations for any changes regarding China: [www.fsis.usda.gov](https://www.fsis.usda.gov)
- Build quality management systems to USDA-equivalent standards as long-term positioning
- Track Section 301 tariff developments through USTR announcements
- Focus resources on accessible markets where regulatory progress is measurable
- Consider that some US food manufacturers use imported egg products as **ingredients** — if a third country with FSIS equivalence processes Chinese eggs, the re-exported product may face fewer barriers (though this adds complexity and cost)
