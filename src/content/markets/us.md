## Market Access Overview

The United States presents a **structurally closed** market for Chinese egg products. The primary barrier is the requirement for USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) equivalence determination, which China has not obtained for egg products. This means Chinese egg products cannot legally enter the US market for human consumption through commercial channels.

Beyond the regulatory barrier, significant **tariff layers** further complicate any future market entry.

### Why "Closed"

- **No FSIS equivalence** — USDA has not determined China's egg product inspection system as equivalent
- **Dual regulatory gate** — Both USDA FSIS (meat/egg inspection) and FDA (facility registration, FSVP) requirements apply
- **Tariff headwinds** — Additional tariffs on Chinese goods materially impact competitiveness
- **Political complexity** — US-China trade relations add uncertainty to regulatory progress

## Regulatory Requirements (If Access Were Granted)

### USDA FSIS Side
1. Country-level equivalence determination for egg product inspection system
2. Individual facility approval by FSIS
3. Continuous FSIS oversight and re-evaluation
4. US-format health certificates for each shipment

### FDA Side
1. Facility registration with FDA (required for all food manufacturers exporting to US)
2. Prior notice for food shipments (minimum 15 days before arrival)
3. Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) compliance by US importer
4. Labeling per FDA requirements (English, nutrition facts, allergen declaration)

## Regulatory Authorities

| Authority | Role |
|-----------|------|
| USDA FSIS | Egg product inspection equivalence, facility approval |
| FDA | Facility registration, prior notice, FSVP, labeling |
| CBP (Customs and Border Protection) | Border enforcement, tariff collection |

## Risk Notes

- **Do not plan US exports as a feasible near-term strategy** — FSIS equivalence is a multi-year process with no current indication of progress
- **Tariff impact is substantial** — even with regulatory access, competitive pricing would be challenging
- FDA requirements (FSVP, facility registration) are **necessary but not sufficient** — FSIS equivalence is the binding constraint
- **Political environment** affects the pace and likelihood of regulatory progress

## Strategic Considerations

- Monitor USDA FSIS equivalence determinations for any changes regarding China
- Build quality management systems to USDA-equivalent standards as long-term positioning
- Focus on accessible markets where regulatory progress is measurable
- Track tariff policy changes that could affect future competitiveness calculations
